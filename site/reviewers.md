# For reviewers

Thank you for trying this. Class 1 is a complete draft: twelve pages and twelve videos.
Nobody outside the project has followed it yet. You are the first.

## What we most want to know

1. **Where did you get stuck?** The exact step, what you typed or clicked, and what you saw.
   A photo of the screen is plenty.
2. **Where were you lost or bored?** A sentence you read twice. A stretch of video you
   skipped.
3. **Does the voice work?** It is generated, in the course author's own voice. Tell us if
   the pace is wrong, if a word is said oddly, or if it is tiring to listen to.
4. **Is anything simply wrong?** A screen that does not look like the page says. A command
   that answers differently.

Small things are welcome. So is "this whole lecture should be shorter".

## Two ways to review

**Read and watch.** You do not need the game. Read a page, watch its video, tell us what
you think. Half an hour a lecture.

**Follow along.** Do what the page says on your own computer. This is the review that
finds the most. You need:

- Artemis Cosmos installed. The lessons are written for version 1.4.0, which is not
  released yet. *[How reviewers get a build that works is being settled. Until it is, the
  follow-along review starts at Lecture 2 and may stop at Lecture 3; tell us where.]*
- About an hour a lecture.

## How to tell us

At the bottom of every lecture page is a link, **Report a problem with this lecture**. It
opens a short form on GitHub (you need a free GitHub account). One report for each thing
is easiest for us; a single long one is fine too.

## What we already know is rough

Please do not spend your time on these:

- **Pictures are missing from the pages.** A line in square brackets such as
  *[Screenshot to add: ...]* marks where one will go.
- **The pictures do not move much.** The videos are made from real screens, as stills with
  a slow push-in. Typing and clicking are shown by their result.
- **Some screens are not a student's.** The game's mission list in Lecture 1 is a
  developer's, with more missions than you will have. A few game pictures show a folder
  named `_pic_probe`.
- **Version numbers** on screen may be newer or older than yours.
- **Lecture 6** was measured on a slightly different starting file from the one Lecture 5
  leaves you with. One of its five examples prints more findings than the page shows.
- **Classes 2 to 6** are not published. Their drafts are in the repository for the curious.

## What happens to your report

Every report is read. Things that are wrong get fixed in the page and, where it changes
what is said or shown, in the video. Things that are a matter of taste are collected until
the first round of reviews is in, then decided together.
